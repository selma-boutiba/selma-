\subsection{Convergence under occlusion}
\label{sec:occlusion}
%%% Referenced from 4.6.4 and from the corrected 4.7.1.

The three variants are now compared on the occluded target. This is the
configuration for which the weighting was introduced, and the one in which the
three schemes are expected to separate. The reference image is acquired from
the unoccluded target, while the images acquired during servoing carry the
opaque elliptical region described in
Section~\ref{sec:test-configurations}, so that every measurement falling
inside it is in error and the error is systematic rather than random: the
occluder does not add noise to the cost, it displaces the pose at which the
cost is least. The poses, the filter bank, the gain schedule and the iteration
cap are unchanged from Section~\ref{sec:nominal}, and the occluder covers
$7.4\%$ of the measurements at the initial pose and $5.80\%$ at the desired
pose, the difference following from the retreat in depth between them.

\paragraph{Results:}
Figures~\ref{fig:occ_v1}--\ref{fig:occ_v3} show the camera velocities, the
positioning error and the feature error against iteration, and
Table~\ref{tab:Occlusion_pose} collects the final pose errors. All three runs
converge in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:metrics}, and none diverges or
oscillates; the separation between them is entirely a separation in accuracy.

The separation is large. The unweighted scheme settles $1291\,\mu\mathrm{m}$
and $1.695^{\circ}$ from the desired pose, against $2.9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ and
$0.016^{\circ}$ for Tukey weighting and $3.1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ and
$0.015^{\circ}$ for the Hermite-informed weighting: a factor of some $440$ in
translation and $110$ in rotation between the baseline and either weighted
variant. Under occlusion, and in contrast to the nominal case of
Section~\ref{sec:nominal}, the cost of weighting a clean scene is repaid many
times over. The contribution of robust weighting to a direct scheme is
therefore not marginal but decisive, and this is the principal quantitative
result of the chapter.

\paragraph{The unweighted scheme minimises a displaced cost.}
The manner of the baseline's failure is more instructive than its magnitude,
and it is the clearest demonstration in this thesis of why a direct scheme
requires robustification. Variant~1 does not fail to converge, nor does it
come to rest at a higher value of the cost than the weighted variants. It
attains the \emph{smallest} final feature error of the three,
$1.551\times10^{10}$ against $1.573\times10^{10}$ for Variant~3,
%%% <-- CONFIRM, and add Variant 2's value; the sentence needs all three

while its pose error is larger by a factor of four hundred. The occluded
measurements have not raised the cost at the correct pose; they have moved the
pose at which the cost is least. Variant~1 finds that minimum accurately and
it is the wrong one. Two consequences follow. The first is practical: no
criterion computed from the same corrupted measurements that drive the
control can detect this failure, which is the argument of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics} met as a measurement. The second is that the
comparison vindicates the formulation of the problem in
Section~\ref{sec:robust-framework} as one of estimation rather than of
optimisation: the difficulty is not that the cost is hard to minimise but that
minimising it is the wrong thing to do.

\paragraph{The two weighted variants are not separated by this experiment.}
Variants~2 and~3 differ by $7.7\%$ in translation, in favour of Variant~2, and
by $7.4\%$ in rotation, in favour of Variant~3. The two differences are of the
same size and point in opposite directions, and each rests on a single run from
a single initial pose. By the standard adopted in Section~\ref{sec:nominal},
which declined to rank the three variants on differences of a few tenths of a
degree, this is a tie, and it is reported as one. The structure-informed
weighting is not shown by this experiment to position more accurately than
generic Tukey weighting under occlusion.

What the experiment does show is that the two reach the same accuracy by
different routes, and the route matters for the reason given in
Section~\ref{sec:hermite-weight-limitation}: the structure-normalised weighting
suppresses measurements because they carry no usable image structure, whereas
the residual-based weighting suppresses them because they disagree with the
model, and only the first of these is a property of the image rather than of
the current pose estimate. Section~\ref{sec:weight-maps} examines the two
mechanisms directly. A stronger claim than parity would require, at a minimum,
the study over several initial poses identified in Section~\ref{sec:future},
and a weight map for Variant~2, which was not recorded.

\paragraph{A remark on the two configurations.}
The final errors of Table~\ref{tab:Occlusion_pose} are some two to three orders
of magnitude smaller than those of Table~\ref{tab:ch4-nominal}, and the two
tables must not be read as though the occluded runs were the more accurate in
any useful sense. The explanation lies in the adaptive gain, and it is the same
mechanism identified in Section~\ref{sec:nominal}. There the run was found to
stall not because the residual had been minimised but because the gain decayed
with it: $\lambda\propto\norm{\mathbf{e}_I}^{2}$ by~\eqref{eq:lambda}, and the
commanded velocity is proportional to the residual in addition, so
$\norm{\mathbf{V}_c}$ falls roughly as the cube of the error and the servo
stalls while a small misalignment remains. Under occlusion the intensity error
does not decay to zero: the reference contains no counterpart to the occluder,
so a residual of fixed magnitude persists over some six per cent of the image
however well the camera is positioned. The gain is computed from that intensity
error and is therefore held up by it,
%%% <-- CONFIRM that lambda is computed from the raw intensity error and
%%% not from the weighted one. The whole paragraph rests on it, and it is
%%% one line of the implementation.
while the weighting removes the same measurements from the descent direction.
The servo consequently continues to drive on the valid measurements at an
undiminished gain, the cubic collapse of the nominal case is replaced by a
velocity proportional to the error alone, and the run converges geometrically
until it meets the limits of the simulation rather than the limits of the gain
schedule.

Three observations follow, and together they account for the whole of
Table~\ref{tab:Occlusion_pose}. First, the micrometre accuracies are a property
of the gain schedule under a persistent residual and not evidence that
occlusion assists convergence; the comparison that matters is between the three
variants of this table, all of which were run under identical conditions, and
not between the two tables. Second, the same mechanism explains why Variant~1
fails as sharply as it does rather than merely drifting: it too enjoys the
sustained gain, and it uses it to converge efficiently upon the displaced
minimum. Third, the observation suggests that the stall reported in
Section~\ref{sec:nominal} is a limitation of the gain schedule inherited from
the implementation rather than of the weighting or of the features, since
removing the decay of $\lambda$ --- by whatever means --- improved the final
accuracy by three orders of magnitude. A gain schedule that does not vanish
with the error is accordingly identified in Section~\ref{sec:future} as the
most immediate improvement available to the scheme.
